RoboVista: Evaluating Vision Language Models for Diverse Robot Applications

Introduction

Deploying robots in real-world domains such as industrial automation, agriculture, and surgery requires a general-purpose interface that can reason over diverse decisions, embodiments, and situations. Vision–Language Models (VLMs) are a promising backbone, but supporting real robot tasks demands consistent, spatially grounded decision-making—for example, judging whether a gasket is properly seated on an assembly line and choosing the correct next action.

Visual Question Answering (VQA) is a primary way to evaluate these capabilities: models receive images or videos and answer task-relevant questions that probe scene understanding and action selection. Existing robot-centric benchmarks often draw heavily from imitation-learning trajectories. Many real applications are modular: behaviors decompose into task-level decisions (sequencing, planning, recovery) that are hard to capture with end-to-end trajectories alone. We extend evaluation to these modular decisions across broader domains—especially where public data is scarce and critical decisions are handled by analytical pipelines.

We propose Robot Question Answering (RQA), a modular evaluation framework, and RoboVista, a benchmark curated from real robotic systems, research papers, and expert annotations. RoboVista contains 474 multiple-choice VQAs with human-annotated reasoning, covering 39 unique task types across agricultural, industrial, domestic, and surgical robotics, autonomous driving, and open robot datasets. Experiments show that state-of-the-art VLMs still exhibit substantial gaps; physical robot experiments suggest strong correlation between RoboVista performance and real-world task execution.

RQA Framework

RQA systematically converts decision points from robot systems into a robot-centric VQA formulation. It mirrors modular robot pipelines: perception, high-level decision making, motion and action estimation, and failure recovery. Perception probes geometry, spatial relations, and physical interaction from robot-centric views. High-level decision making requires grounded task reasoning—goals, sequencing, and next steps under constraints. Motion and action awareness tests feasibility (reachability, collision, closed-loop feedback). Recovery and robustness assess failure detection and corrective actions.

Together, these stages provide a structured abstraction for diverse reasoning problems in a unified Robot–VQA interface, combining expert annotation, algorithmic execution, and automated question construction where appropriate.

RoboVista Benchmark

RoboVista is an expert-annotated, robot-centric VQA benchmark. Each question is grounded in robot-visible or onboard visual observations and paired with detailed human reasoning. To ensure realism and consistency, questions are constructed and verified by human domain experts familiar with the underlying algorithms and task constraints.

  • Scale: 474 multiple-choice questions with annotated reasoning.
  • Coverage: 39 distinct task types across six areas—agriculture, industry, domestic settings, surgical robotics, autonomous driving, and open robot datasets.
  • Focus: Fine-grained spatial understanding, embodied decision-making, variable embodiments, deformable objects and cluttered scenes, and long-horizon contextual understanding.

Evaluation and Results

We evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs on RoboVista with techniques such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and In-Context Learning (ICL). Results indicate substantial headroom across many application domains. Beyond the benchmark, we report physical validation—including a bi-manual spatial estimation task using domain-specific object geometry—and closed-loop VLM-guided surgical knot tying. Degradations in RoboVista scores align with reduced long-horizon task success, supporting RoboVista as a meaningful indicator of embodied performance.

Benchmark results

Frontier VLMs are benchmarked on the full RoboVista suite with standardized prompting and optional reasoning strategies. Cross-domain analysis highlights where spatial grounding, long-horizon context, and failure-aware reasoning remain challenging relative to human expert performance.

Physical validation

We complement the dataset with physical experiments: a bi-manual spatial estimation task that uses domain-specific object geometry as priors, and closed-loop VLM-guided surgical knot tying. Together, these studies suggest that RoboVista tracks task-level success in the real world—when RoboVista performance drops, long-horizon behavior degrades accordingly.

Conclusion

Our contributions are:

  1. RQA — a modular framework for constructing robot-centric VQAs from expert annotation, demonstration, and execution.
  2. RoboVista — a benchmark spanning diverse applications, scenes, and embodiments.
  3. Analysis — comprehensive evaluation of frontier VLMs, including cross-domain analysis, training studies, and complementary physical-robot validation.

We hope RoboVista and RQA help the community align VLMs with the modular structure of real robotic systems and close the gap between visual reasoning and reliable physical behavior.
